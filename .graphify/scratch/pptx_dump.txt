Care a patient can't act on isn't really care
56%
of NZ adults — over 1.6 million people — can't obtain and understand basic health information well enough to make informed decisions.
~90%
of Pacific people aged 15+ have low health literacy. Māori and Pacific rates sit below non-Māori.
16.1%
of acute medical admissions in over-65s are readmitted within 30 days — higher for Māori, Pacific, and deprived areas.
Killer data point:  an LLM-rewritten discharge summary moved understandability from 13% to 81% — the same study also introduced errors, which is exactly why our design puts a clinician in the middle.
--- Slide 4 ---
THE REFRAME
This isn't a nice-to-have. It's a legal right.
[IMAGE]
Right 5 — Code of Health & Disability Services Consumers' Rights
Every consumer has the right to effective communication “in a form, language, and manner that enables the consumer to understand.” A consent form the patient didn't understand is arguably not compliant. Right 6 adds the right to be fully informed.
Why that changes the pitch
[IMAGE]
From helpful to required
Judges can verify the obligation exists — it's law, not opinion.
[IMAGE]
A defensible reason to exist
We're closing a compliance gap, not adding a chatbot.
[IMAGE]
Local and specific
This is the NZ Code — overseas products aren't built around it.

HOW IT WORKS  ·  BUILDABLE IN A DAY
Four steps. No training, no hospital integration.
1
[IMAGE]
Ingest & structure
Pull the parts a patient acts on from a PDF: meds & changes, warning signs, follow-ups, who to call.
2
[IMAGE]
Rewrite + clinician gate
Generate a plain-language version at a target reading level. A nurse approves or edits before release.
3
[IMAGE]
Visual breakdown
Medication timeline, a red-flag 'call now' card, and a follow-up calendar. Structure does the work.
4
[IMAGE]
Language & format
Te reo Māori, Pacific languages, large print, audio — with human verification. Where Right 5 is met.
Why 48 hours works:  PDF extraction + one structured-extraction call + one rewrite call + a review screen. The review UI is ordinary CRUD. Nothing to train, nothing to integrate.
--- Slide 7 ---
DIFFERENTIATION
“Why not just use ChatGPT — or Epic?”
[IMAGE]
Epic's Emmie exists — but
It summarises lab & procedure results inside MyChart. It has no shipped feature that rewrites the full inpatient discharge summary or the consent form.
[IMAGE]
NZ public hospitals don't run on Epic
So none of that reaches a patient at Waikato Hospital. The gap is real and local.
[IMAGE]
No overseas product does te reo Māori or Pacific languages
That output — the part where Right 5 is actually met for NZ — is ours to own.
[IMAGE]
The clinician gate is the difference
A generic chatbot ships its guess straight to the patient. We put a human in the middle by design.
